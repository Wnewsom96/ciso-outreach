# Iteration Playbook — What to Do After Outreach Goes Out

This file is a reference for the `/ciso-outreach` skill Step 4 (Reply Handling). Read it when the user comes back with what happened after sending.

---

## How to Use This File

The user will paste one of five scenarios:
1. No reply after X days
2. A positive reply (asked a question, said yes to a call, forwarded to someone)
3. A soft negative ("not now," "not the right time," "reach out in Q4")
4. A hard negative ("not interested," "remove me," "we're all set")
5. An out-of-office or automated response

Diagnose the signal, then produce the single next move. Do not produce another full outreach package. One action at a time.

---

## Scenario 1 — No Reply

The user says: "Sent Email 1 on [date]. No reply."

### Diagnose first

Ask:
- Which email in the sequence did they send?
- Was it opened? (If they have tracking — Outreach, Salesloft, HubSpot, Yesware — this matters)
- How many days since they sent it?
- Did they send only email or also LinkedIn?

### Decision tree

**Email opened, no reply, under 5 days:**
Too early. Don't follow up yet. Wait until Day 4–5 for Email 2. Reply with: "You're in the normal window. Send Email 2 on [date]."

**Email opened multiple times, no reply, Day 4–5:**
They read it and didn't reply — which means the hook wasn't strong enough to pull a reply, but they were interested enough to open it more than once. Email 2 (Peer Proof) is the right move. Write a variation where the subject line references the specific content of Email 1 to confirm it's a continuation and not a new thread.

**Email not opened, Day 3–4:**
The subject line didn't work. Try a new subject line approach — shorter, more specific to their company, or referencing a recent event. Rewrite Email 1 with an alternate subject line. The body can stay the same or change the first sentence.

**Alternate subject line options by situation:**

Original was industry-generic:
> Try: "[Company] specifically — [one-word topic]"

Original referenced a conference talk:
> Try: "[Year] RSA → [one word they said]" or just their first name and one word from their talk

Original referenced a peer company:
> Try: "Re: [peer company name]" — use "Re:" even though it's a new thread; it reads like a follow-up to something they know

**No reply on LinkedIn connection request, 5+ days:**
They haven't accepted. Either they haven't seen it, or they saw it and didn't connect. Move to email. If email address is available, send Email 1 there. Reference in the email that you sent a LinkedIn request — this shows persistence without being aggressive.

**No reply after full 4-email sequence:**
This account is not dead — it's in cooldown. Note the date. Set a re-entry date for 60–90 days out. When you come back, the rule is: new angle, new trigger, never reference that you already reached out. Start as if it's a new approach anchored to something new (a recent company announcement, a new regulation, a new breach in their sector).

## Scenario 2 — Positive Reply

A positive reply is not just "yes to a call." Positive replies come in several forms, each requiring a different response.

### "I'm interested — when are you free?"

This is a meeting acceptance. Do not over-respond. Reply with two times:
```
[Name] — great. [Day] at [Time Zone] or [Day] at [Time Zone] work for me.
Here's my calendar if easier: [link]
[Your name]
```

Do not re-pitch in this email. They already said yes. Every additional sentence risks muddying the reason they agreed to meet.

### "Interesting — tell me more"

They want more information before committing to a call. This is not a yes to a meeting yet. Send a one-paragraph reply — specific to what they said — and end with a soft ask for 20 minutes.

Template:
```
[Name] — [one sentence directly answering what they're implicitly asking about].

[One sentence on the specific outcome for a company in their position].

Worth 20 minutes to walk through exactly how that works? Happy to send a specific time or share my calendar.

[Your name]
```

Do not send a deck. Do not send a case study PDF. They asked for more — answer in text. If they want a deck, they'll ask.

### "Can you send me some information?"

This is a deflection, not a request for information. They're testing whether you'll just dump a brochure on them or whether you'll actually engage.

Reply:
```
[Name] — happy to. So I can send you something actually worth reading — what's the most pressing security challenge you're working on right now?

[Your name]
```

Wait for their answer. Their answer tells you the real problem and the real pitch. Then send one page of relevant information, specific to what they said. Not a product sheet. Not a deck. One paragraph or a one-page asset.

### "Forward this to [person/team]"

They're not the buyer. Or they are the buyer and they want someone else to evaluate. Either way, this is a warm introduction — the best kind.

Email the referred person and reference the original contact by name:
```
Subject: [Original contact's first name] thought we should connect

[Referred name] — [Original contact] mentioned I should reach out. We've been discussing [one-line description of the topic] and they thought it was worth getting your perspective.

[One sentence on the specific relevance to the referred person's role].

Happy to set up 20 minutes with both of you or just the two of us first — whatever's easier. [Calendar link]

[Your name]
```

Then go back to the intelligence report and research the referred person. They are now the entry point.

### "I actually have a renewal coming up in [timeframe]"

This is a buying window disclosure. They told you exactly when to be there.

Reply warmly:
```
[Name] — that's helpful to know. Most of the [industry] teams we work with use the 60 days before renewal to get an independent look at what else is out there — so when the conversation starts internally, they're not starting from scratch.

Worth a conversation in the next few weeks so you have that context before the cycle starts?

[Your name]
```

Set a follow-up reminder for 45 days before their stated renewal date. That's when to send the next email.

## Scenario 3 — Soft Negative

The user says: "They replied — not right now, maybe Q4, reach out later."

### Do not:
- Promise to reach out at a specific date and then forget
- Ask them to define "later" — they already gave you a window

### Do:

Reply with one line acknowledging the timing and a specific re-entry date:
```
[Name] — makes sense. I'll circle back in [month] with something specific.

[Your name]
```

Note the re-entry date. When you come back:
- Reference that they asked you to reach back out: "You mentioned [month] — here I am."
- Anchor to something new that happened between then and now: a new incident in their sector, a regulation update, a new company announcement
- Do not repeat the same pitch. Assume they'll remember what you sent. Come back with a new angle.

**If they said "Q4" specifically:**
Q4 for most enterprises means October–November budget conversations. Come back in late September with something specific to their fiscal year buying cycle.

**If they said "next year":**
That's a 6-month+ cooldown. Put the account in monitoring. Set an alert for any new critical signal (CISO change, breach, major hire surge, M&A). Re-enter when a new signal fires — don't just wait for the calendar date.

## Scenario 4 — Hard Negative

The user says: "They said not interested / remove me / we're all set."

### First: read the signal accurately

"Not interested" means different things depending on what came before it:
- After Email 1 cold outreach: standard friction, not a real objection. Most reps stop here. Don't.
- After a call or meeting: real disqualification. Respect it and exit cleanly.
- After a "remove me" with no prior engagement: they don't want outreach from you. Respect it and remove them. Do not circle back.

### For "not interested" after Email 1 (no prior meeting):

One more reply — not to overcome the objection, but to change the angle:
```
[Name] — totally fair. Just curious — is it the timing, or is [product category] not on the radar at all for this year?

[Your name]
```

Two outcomes:
- They answer: you have more information. Use it.
- They don't reply: the account goes into 90-day cooldown. That's fine.

Never send more than one follow-up after a "not interested." Two follow-ups after a rejection signals that you're not listening. CISOs talk to each other.

### For "remove me":

Remove them. No reply. No follow-up. If they've explicitly asked to be removed from outreach, honor it immediately. Continuing to reach out after a remove request is a compliance issue (CAN-SPAM, GDPR) and a reputation issue. The security community is smaller than it looks.

### For "we're all set / we have something for that":

This is not a hard no — it's a status update. They have an incumbent. They're locked in.

Reply:
```
[Name] — understood. Quick question before I go — when does your current contract renew?

[Your name]
```

If they answer: note the renewal date. Set a re-entry 60 days before it. Come back with a specific comparison or displacement play.

If they don't answer: put the account in cooldown. Set a 90-day check on the account for any signals that the incumbent relationship is weakening (job posting for a competing tool, security engineer departure, breach incident, compliance failure).

## Scenario 5 — Out-of-Office or Auto-Response

The user says: "Got an auto-reply — they're out until [date]."

No action needed until they're back. Note the return date. Send Email 2 the day after they return. If the out-of-office mentions who to contact in their absence, evaluate whether that person is worth reaching out to — if they're a direct report or a security team lead, yes. If they're an EA or admin, no.

---

## Reading Aggregate Signal Across the Sequence

After the full 4-email sequence, the open/reply pattern tells you which angle worked best:

| Pattern | What It Means |
|---|---|
| Email 1 opened many times, no reply | The subject worked, the body didn't pull a reply — add more specificity or a harder ask in the re-enter |
| Email 2 got a reply | Peer proof is the right angle for this account — lead with case studies and outcomes in re-entry |
| Email 3 got a reply | Industry threat / regulatory urgency resonates — come back with more sector-specific intel |
| Nothing opened | The email is landing in spam, the wrong inbox, or the contact info is wrong — try LinkedIn or a different email format |
| LinkedIn got a reply but email got nothing | This person prefers LinkedIn — shift all future touches to that channel |

Use the pattern to choose the angle on re-entry. If Email 2 worked for one person in a company, it's likely to work for another person in the same account.

---

## Re-Entry Principles

When coming back to an account after a cooldown:

1. New signal required — do not re-enter without a specific new reason. A new breach in their sector, a new regulation, a new CISO hire, a new funding round, a new job posting. "I just wanted to follow up" is not a reason.

2. Never reference the prior outreach unless they specifically asked you to come back. Start fresh. If they did ask you to come back, reference that in the first line.

3. New angle — if the first run was a pen testing pitch, come back with the compliance angle. If it was the compliance angle, come back with a peer company story. Same product, different pain frame.

4. 90-day minimum cooldown for soft negatives. 180-day minimum for hard negatives. No cooldown required if a new critical signal fires.
