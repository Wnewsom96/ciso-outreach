# CISO Psychology — How Security Executives Think and Buy

This file is a reference for the `/ciso-outreach` skill. Read it before scoring buying signals and before writing outreach copy.

---

## What a CISO Actually Cares About

CISOs operate under three simultaneous pressures. Every outreach message that doesn't acknowledge at least one of these gets ignored.

**1. Board accountability**
The CISO's job tenure depends on two things: not having a breach on their watch, and being able to explain security posture to a board that doesn't understand security. They need to translate risk into business language. Products that help them do that — or that reduce the risk of the career-ending breach — get attention.

**2. Regulatory and compliance burden**
Most CISOs spend 30–40% of their time on compliance. HIPAA, PCI-DSS, SOX, CMMC, NERC CIP, NY DFS, SEC cyber disclosure rules, GDPR — these are mandates with deadlines and teeth. A product that helps them close a compliance gap is a product with a built-in budget line.

**3. Doing more with fewer people**
Security teams are chronically understaffed. The average enterprise security team handles far more than it can staff for. Anything that reduces manual work, consolidates vendors, or helps a small team cover more ground gets a conversation.

## What CISOs Do NOT Care About (in outreach)

- Feature lists. They have engineers for that.
- Analyst rankings and awards. They've read the same Gartner quadrant you're referencing.
- Generic ROI claims without specifics tied to their situation.
- "Industry-leading" anything.
- How many customers you have unless a specific peer company name is included.

## The Buying Trigger Framework

Score each account against these triggers. The more triggers present, the higher the urgency.

### CRITICAL (act now — highest priority accounts)

These represent open buying windows. If any of these are present, move to this account immediately.

| Trigger | What It Means | Where to Find It |
|---|---|---|
| New CISO (under 90 days in role) | New CISOs buy. They're building their program, setting their agenda, and have political cover to change vendors. The window closes around month 6. | LinkedIn job change notifications, company press release, Google news |
| Active breach or public incident | The company just had a breach. Board is demanding answers. Budget is suddenly available. | SEC 8-K filings, HaveIBeenPwned, news coverage, CISA advisories |
| Board-level security mandate disclosed | Company has publicly stated security is a board priority — often in earnings calls or annual reports. | Earnings transcripts, annual reports, 10-K |
| Regulatory action or fine | Company received a regulatory enforcement action related to security. | FTC, HHS, OCC, FINRA enforcement databases, news |
| Compliance certification deadline | CMMC deadline for a defense contractor, HIPAA audit cycle, PCI recertification coming up. | Industry calendars, job postings mentioning specific frameworks |

### STRONG (reach out this week)

Elevated buying probability. Not a guaranteed open window, but someone is thinking about security right now.

| Trigger | What It Means | Where to Find It |
|---|---|---|
| Major security hiring surge | 3+ open security roles in the past 60 days | LinkedIn jobs, Indeed, Glassdoor |
| M&A activity | Acquiring or being acquired means two security environments need to merge | Crunchbase, news |
| New compliance requirement | A new regulation just passed that affects their industry | Industry trade press |
| Peer company breach | A direct competitor or industry peer just had a breach | News — CISOs watch each other |
| Job posting lists your product's competitor | Budget exists and awareness exists. Displace the incumbent. | LinkedIn jobs, job descriptions mentioning Crowdstrike, Palo Alto, Zscaler, etc. |
| CISO posting publicly about a specific security challenge | They've told you the problem — pitch the solution | LinkedIn, X/Twitter |

### MODERATE (add to cadence now, follow up in 30 days)

Worth pursuing but no immediate buying window identified.

| Trigger | What It Means | Where to Find It |
|---|---|---|
| Speaking at a security conference | They're publicly visible — good hook for outreach | RSA, Black Hat, ISACA, local BSides speaker lists |
| Published an article or research paper | They've articulated a security belief publicly — engage with it | Dark Reading, CSO Online, LinkedIn articles |
| Company just announced a major product launch | New product = new attack surface = new security conversations | Company blog, press releases |
| Funding round closed | Series B or later means a security budget line is likely being built | Crunchbase, AngelList, news |

### WEAK (monitor, low priority)

Present but not actionable on their own.

| Trigger | What It Means |
|---|---|
| Generic digital transformation news | Everyone is transforming. This alone is not a buying signal. |
| New CEO or COO hire | Useful context, not a direct security buying signal unless it changes the board's priorities |
| Company rebranding | No security buying signal |
| General LinkedIn activity with no specific security content | They're active but not necessarily in buying mode |

## How CISOs Evaluate Vendors

Understanding this shapes how you pitch.

**They buy trust before they buy products.**
A cold email from an unknown rep asking for a demo gets ignored. A cold email from a rep who clearly did homework — referencing a real problem the CISO is facing, naming a real peer company, and asking for a conversation not a demo — gets a reply.

**They do not respond to urgency manufactured by the seller.**
"End of quarter," "limited time offer," "this deal expires Friday" — these phrases end relationships with CISOs. They know what those mean. Do not use them.

**They evaluate vendors by how they behave before the sale.**
If your outreach is generic, they assume your support will be generic. If your outreach is specific, researched, and relevant, they assume your team pays attention. The outreach IS the product demo.

**They protect their team's time.**
Getting past a CISO's admin or EA requires a specific, plausible reason for the call — not a pitch. "I want to share what three healthcare CISOs did with their HIPAA audit prep this year" is a specific reason. "I want to show you our platform" is not.

**Technical CISOs (former engineers, OSCP, GIAC, GitHub profiles) evaluate differently.**
They will google your product before the first call. They will ask technical questions immediately. They do not want the marketing deck. Lead with technical depth and real specifics. Skip the feature-to-benefit translation layer — they'll do it themselves.

## The Buying Committee — Who to Contact First

Never start with the CISO cold unless you have a specific signal-based reason. The best entry sequence:

**1. Head of AppSec / VP Security Engineering / Director of Threat Intelligence**
This person lives in the daily pain. They understand the problem technically. They can become a champion. They have access to the CISO. Start here for most cybersecurity products.

**2. CISO**
Economic buyer. Signs budget. Needs to see ROI in board-level language. Do not cold-pitch a CISO on features. Pitch business risk, peer benchmarking, or compliance outcomes. Contact after you have a champion inside, or when a critical trigger is present.

**3. CTO / VP Engineering**
For developer security, supply chain security, or DevSecOps products — the CTO or VP Eng often controls the budget and the developer platform. Engage alongside AppSec for DevOps-led conversations.

**4. Security Architect / Principal Security Engineer**
Technical evaluator. Runs the POC. Becomes a secondary champion. Engage after the first meeting.

**5. Procurement / Legal**
Paper process only. Never a starting point.

## The New CISO Window — The Highest-Value Signal in Security Sales

A CISO who has been in role fewer than 90 days is the single highest-converting target in enterprise security sales.

Why:
- They are building their program and making vendor decisions
- They have political cover to change what the previous CISO chose
- They are meeting with lots of vendors and are open to conversations
- They want to show the board they are moving quickly
- They haven't locked in relationships yet

How to approach:
- Acknowledge they're new without making it awkward
- Frame the conversation around helping them build the program, not selling a point solution
- Offer to share what peers in their industry are doing — not a product pitch
- The ask is a conversation, not a demo

Example first sentence for a new CISO:
"Saw you recently stepped into the CISO role at [Company] — congratulations. I work with a few [industry] CISOs who are navigating similar program-building decisions. Happy to share what they're prioritizing if useful."

## Industry-Specific CISO Pain Points

Use these to write industry-targeted outreach.

**Healthcare**
Primary driver: HIPAA compliance, OCR audits, ransomware targeting patient data, HITRUST certification requirements, breach notification liability.
Hot topics: Third-party vendor risk (BAAs), medical device security, EHR system security.
Frame your product around: Patient data protection, audit-readiness, reducing breach notification liability.

**Financial Services (Banking, Insurance, Wealth Management)**
Primary driver: SOX, PCI-DSS, NY DFS 500, OCC requirements, FFIEC guidance, insider threat.
Hot topics: Cloud migration security, third-party risk, fraud detection, zero-trust architecture.
Frame your product around: Regulatory compliance, audit evidence, fraud prevention, examiner readiness.

**Manufacturing / Industrial / OT**
Primary driver: NERC CIP (utilities), CMMC (defense contractors), IP theft, operational technology security.
Hot topics: IT/OT convergence, ransomware on production lines, supply chain attacks.
Frame your product around: Operational continuity, protecting manufacturing IP, CMMC certification.

**Retail / E-commerce**
Primary driver: PCI-DSS, customer data breaches, payment fraud, supply chain attacks on digital storefronts.
Hot topics: Web skimming, third-party JavaScript risk, API security, loyalty program fraud.
Frame your product around: Customer trust, PCI compliance, brand reputation protection.

**Technology / SaaS**
Primary driver: Software supply chain security, customer trust, FedRAMP (if selling to government), SOC 2 compliance.
Hot topics: Developer security (shifting left), open source risk, API security, cloud misconfigurations.
Frame your product around: Developer velocity without sacrificing security, customer trust, FedRAMP readiness.

**Government / Defense**
Primary driver: CMMC, FISMA, FedRAMP, classified system security, insider threat.
Hot topics: Zero trust architecture (mandated by EO 14028), supply chain risk management (SCRM).
Frame your product around: CMMC compliance milestones, zero trust implementation, audit evidence generation.

**Education / Higher Education**
Primary driver: FERPA, research data protection, ransomware (frequent target), state-level regulations.
Hot topics: Ransomware recovery, student data protection, research IP protection.
Frame your product around: Student data protection, ransomware resilience, research security.
