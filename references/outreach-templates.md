# Outreach Templates and Copy Rules — CISO Outreach

This file is a reference for the `/ciso-outreach` skill. Read it before writing any outreach copy.

---

## Universal Copy Rules

Apply these to every piece of copy — email, LinkedIn, cold call, direct mail.

**Never use these words:**
leverage, utilize, facilitate, robust, holistic, synergy, streamline, empower, revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, transformative, innovative, ecosystem, solutions, seamlessly, comprehensive, best-in-class, industry-leading, next-generation

**Never open with:**
- "I hope this finds you well"
- "In today's fast-paced world"
- "As a [title], you know..."
- "I wanted to reach out because..."
- "My name is X and I work for Y" as the first sentence

**Structural rules:**
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- No em dashes.
- No bullet point dumps in email copy — it reads like a brochure.
- End with one clear ask, not multiple options or questions.
- The subject line is not the pitch — it is the reason they open it.
- Every piece of copy must contain at least one specific reference to them personally or to their company specifically.

**Tone calibration:**
- Technical CISO (OSCP, GIAC, GitHub, former engineer): strip it down further. No fluff at all. Skip analogies. Be blunt.
- Non-technical CISO (legal or finance background, board-level operator): slightly more polished, emphasize risk language and board optics.
- Academic / thought leader CISO (professor, author, conference speaker): engage with their ideas before pitching. Reference their work.
- Former vendor CISO (was a CISO-in-Residence or advisor at a security company): they know every play. Be disarmingly honest about what you're doing. "I'm going to pitch you. Here's why it's worth your time."

## Email Sequence — Detailed Guidance

### Email 1 — Signal Anchor

**Goal:** Get the open. Get a reply. Not a meeting — a reply.

**Length:** 75–100 words max, not counting subject line.

**Subject line rules:**
- Under 8 words
- Specific — reference their company, their industry, their role, or a recent event
- Never a question mark (questions read as clickbait)
- Never "Following up," "Quick question," or "Touching base"
- Examples of good subject lines:
  - "Healthcare pen test — what Atrium found"
  - "[Company] cloud exposure after the [incident]"
  - "CMMC deadline — what [defense contractor peer] did"
  - "New CISO at [Company] — happy to share what others are doing"

**Body structure:**
Line 1: The hook — one sentence, specific to a signal you found. Not a generic opener.
Line 2: What you do — one sentence, outcome-focused, not product-focused.
Line 3: One sentence of relevance — why this specific company or person.
Line 4: The close — "Happy to share what we're seeing at [peer company type]. Worth a conversation?"

**Examples of strong Email 1 hooks by trigger:**

New CISO signal:
> "Saw you recently stepped into the CISO role at [Company]. I work with a few [industry] CISOs who are in the same spot — building the program, evaluating the stack, deciding what to change. Happy to share what they're prioritizing right now."

Recent sector breach:
> "Three [industry] CISOs called us the week after the [company/incident] news. All of them wanted to run the same test on their environment. Happy to share what they found."

Job posting signal:
> "[Company] is hiring a Cloud Security Engineer — which usually means the team has spotted a gap but doesn't have the cycle time to close it yet. We've helped a few [industry] security teams cover that gap while the hire came onboard. Worth a conversation?"

Compliance deadline:
> "CMMC 2.0 Level 2 deadline is [date] for defense contractors. Three companies in [state or region] have asked us to help them get pen test evidence done before the audit window. If [Company] is on that timeline, worth a quick conversation."

Conference talk:
> "Watched your talk at RSA on [topic]. The point about [specific thing they said] is something we hear from most [industry] CISOs. We've helped a few of them turn that observation into a specific program change. Happy to share how."

---

### Email 2 — Peer Proof

**Goal:** Establish credibility with a specific story. Show that a real peer had the same problem and solved it.

**Length:** 100–125 words max.

**Rules:**
- Name the peer company by name if you have permission. If not, name the industry and company size: "a 3,000-person regional bank" or "a healthcare system similar to yours."
- Include one specific outcome — a number, a timeframe, a before/after. Not "improved their security posture."
- The ask is smaller than a meeting — "worth a 20-minute look" not "can we schedule a demo."

**Template:**
> Subject: Re: [original subject]
>
> [Name] — a [industry] [CISO / VP Security] at a company similar to [their company] was dealing with [specific version of their problem]. They'd [tried X / had X in place] and it wasn't covering [specific gap].
>
> We ran [specific service or assessment] and found [specific outcome or result].
>
> Happy to walk through exactly what we did and what it surfaced. 20 minutes — worth it?
>
> [Your name]

---

### Email 3 — Industry Threat Angle

**Goal:** Create urgency without being fake about it. Reference a real, recent threat or regulatory development in their industry.

**Length:** 75–100 words.

**Rules:**
- This must be a real, recent, specific threat or regulatory event — not a generic "cyber threats are increasing" statement.
- Connect it directly to what you sell. One sentence.
- Do not repeat the same ask format as Email 2. Vary the wording.

**Template:**
> Subject: [Industry] + [specific threat or regulation]
>
> [Name] — [specific recent incident or regulatory change affecting their industry]. [One sentence on what this specifically means for a company in their position.]
>
> [One sentence on how your product/service addresses that exact exposure.]
>
> Worth 20 minutes this week?
>
> [Your name]

**Examples by industry:**

Healthcare:
> "[Hospital system X] filed an HHS breach notification last month — ransomware on the EHR. The attack vector was a third-party vendor with excessive access. We help healthcare security teams identify and close that specific gap before OCR asks. Worth 20 minutes?"

Financial Services:
> "The SEC's new cyber disclosure rules require public financial firms to report material incidents within 4 days. A few bank CISOs we work with are using that deadline to upgrade their detection capabilities — specifically to make sure they can determine materiality fast. Happy to share what that looks like."

Defense / Manufacturing:
> "CMMC 2.0 Level 2 enforcement is coming. DoD contractors who haven't gotten a third-party assessment done are going to have a hard Q4. We're working with three contractors in [region] right now on exactly this. Worth a conversation?"

---

### Email 4 — Break-Up

**Goal:** Give them a final, low-pressure out — and leave one piece of genuine value behind.

**Length:** 3–4 sentences max.

**Rules:**
- No guilt. No "I've tried to reach you several times."
- No urgency pressure.
- Leave one specific, useful resource — a report, a framework, a case study they might actually open.
- End warmly. You want to come back to this account in 90 days.

**Template:**
> Subject: Closing the loop on this
>
> [Name] — I'll leave you alone after this. If [solving specific problem] isn't a priority right now, totally fair.
>
> I'll leave [a specific resource — threat report, framework, relevant article] here in case it's useful: [link]
>
> If anything changes, you know where to find me.
>
> [Your name]

## LinkedIn Sequence — Detailed Guidance

### Touch 1 — Connection Request

**Character limit:** 200 characters (LinkedIn's limit on connection notes).

**Rules:**
- Never pitch in the connection note. Ever.
- One specific reference to them — a post, a talk, a shared connection, a shared school or company, a shared interest.
- End with a simple reason you want to connect — not what you're selling.

**Strong examples:**
> "Your post on [topic] at RSA was one of the better takes I read this year. I work in [cybersecurity sales / security space] and wanted to connect."

> "We both worked at [company] — different times, but I always respect people who came up through that org. Wanted to connect."

> "Saw your [Dark Reading / CSO Online] piece on [topic]. Agree with the [specific point]. Wanted to connect with people thinking about this seriously."

**Weak examples (never use):**
> "Hi [Name], I'd like to connect with you and explore potential synergies."
> "I work at [Company] and would love to discuss how we can help with your security needs."

---

### Touch 2 — Value Message (Day 3 after connecting)

**Goal:** Deliver something useful before asking for anything. Build credibility.

**Length:** 3–4 sentences.

**Rules:**
- Do not pitch.
- Share something they would actually want to read — a relevant threat report, a framework tied to something they've publicly written about, a summary of what similar companies are doing.
- No ask.

**Template:**
> "[Name] — saw your recent post on [topic] and thought this [report / framework / article] was relevant to the point you were making: [link or summary]. [One sentence on what makes it specifically useful to someone in their situation.] Thought it was worth passing along."

---

### Touch 3 — Direct Ask (Day 8 after connecting)

**Goal:** Book the conversation.

**Length:** 2–3 sentences.

**Rules:**
- Acknowledge they're busy — briefly. One phrase, not a paragraph.
- One sentence on what's specifically in it for them. Not "to learn about our product."
- Low-friction ask.

**Template:**
> "[Name] — I know your time is limited. I think 20 minutes talking through what [peer industry type] CISOs are doing with [specific problem area] would be worth it for you. Worth finding a slot?"

## Cold Call Script — Detailed Guidance

### Pre-Call Preparation

Before dialing, have these ready:
1. The specific signal you're referencing (from the intelligence report)
2. One peer company name (same industry, same size, relevant outcome)
3. Two open calendar slots for the ask (Tuesday or Wednesday work best)
4. The voicemail script written out — you will need it

### Full Call Script

**Step 1 — The Permission Opener:**
```
"Hey [Name], this is [Your name] from [Company].
I know I'm calling out of the blue — do you have 30 seconds?"
```

Wait. If they say yes, continue. If they say "what's this about," go to the bridge immediately.

**Step 2 — The Bridge (if they give you 30 seconds):**
Reference the specific signal. Keep it to one sentence.
```
"The reason I'm calling is [specific signal — their recent talk / a breach in their sector /
a job posting / their CISO transition]. I work with [industry] security teams specifically on
[category], and I think there's something worth sharing with you."
```

**Step 3 — The Value Statement:**
One sentence. Outcome-focused. Name a peer company if possible.
```
"We helped [peer company type / specific company if you have it] [specific outcome —
close a compliance gap / cut SOC alert volume in half / identify a cloud misconfiguration
before it was exploited]."
```

**Step 4 — The Ask:**
Not a demo. A conversation.
```
"I'm not trying to sell you anything on this call. I want to earn 20 minutes to show you
what they did. Is [Tuesday / Wednesday] better for a quick call?"
```

### Objection Handlers

**"We already have something for that."**
```
"That makes sense. Most of the CISOs we work with had something in place too —
the question we usually start with is whether it's actually covering the gap
or just checking the box. Would it be worth 20 minutes to look at that together?"
```

**"Send me an email."**
```
"Absolutely. What's the best address? And so I can make the email worth opening —
what's the most pressing security priority you're dealing with right now?"
```
[Get the answer. Send the email the same day. Reference exactly what they said.]

**"Not interested."**
```
"I hear you. Just curious — is it the timing, or is [category] not on the radar at all
for this year?"
```
[If timing: "When would be a better time to come back?" If not on radar: "Fair. Who would be the right person to circle back with on the team?"]

**"We're locked in until [date] / renewal is in [X months]."**
```
"That's actually helpful to know. A lot of what we do is help teams prepare for
that renewal conversation — knowing what else is out there and what the evaluation
should look like. Would it be worth a 20-minute conversation before that cycle starts?"
```

**"I don't take cold calls."**
```
"Fair enough. If I send you one specific piece of information relevant to [their company /
their industry challenge] — would you be willing to look at it?"
```

---

### Voicemail Script

Time it at under 20 seconds. Practice before dialing.

```
"Hey [Name], [Your name] from [Company] — [one specific reference to them or their company].
I'll follow up by email today. You can reach me at [number, say it slowly] —
again that's [number]. Looking forward to connecting."
```

What the one specific reference sounds like:
- "Saw your talk on zero trust at ISACA last month"
- "Wanted to talk to you specifically about what [sector] CISOs are doing with [topic]"
- "Heard about the team you've been building since joining [Company]"

## Direct Mail — Concept Framework

Direct mail is for Tier 1 accounts only. A $50–$200 per-account investment is worth it when the deal size is $500K+.

### What makes a direct mail piece work

**It must be specific to them — not to CISOs in general.**
A generic direct mail piece goes in the trash next to the other vendor swag. A piece that references their specific conference talk, their published book, their college, or their home city stops them.

**It must be memorable and physical.**
The goal is to create a reason to remember your name before the follow-up call or email. It is not to close the deal by mail.

**It must have a clear next step.**
Every direct mail piece ends with one action: visit this URL, reply to this email, or expect a call on [date].

### Direct Mail Concept Templates

**The Book Play:**
When the target has authored a book or published significant research.
```
Artifact: Their own book with a handwritten sticky note on the cover.
Note: "[Name] — read [specific chapter or point] twice. The part about [specific thing]
is exactly what we're seeing at [industry] companies right now.
Would love to share what we're doing about it. Expect an email from me this week.
— [Your name]"
```

**The Industry Report Play:**
When the target is analytically minded and their industry has a relevant recent threat report.
```
Artifact: A printed and bound copy of a relevant threat report (Verizon DBIR,
IBM Cost of a Data Breach, CISA advisory) with your company's cover page and
3–4 highlighted passages specific to their industry.
Note: "Thought this belonged on your desk more than in your inbox.
Page [X] is why I'm reaching out. — [Your name, phone number]"
```

**The Personal Interest Play:**
When the target has a documented personal interest (running, specific sports team, faith community, alma mater).
```
Artifact: Something tied to their interest — a book by an author they've mentioned,
a relevant item tied to their school or team, something from their city.
Note: "Figured this was a better introduction than a cold email.
[One sentence on what you do and what peer company result connects to their situation.]
Expect a call from me [day]. — [Your name]"
```

**The Dimensional Mailer Play:**
When none of the above applies — use the problem itself as the artifact.
```
Artifact: A custom-printed "threat report" for their specific company —
a 1-page summary of publicly visible exposures (Shodan, job posting signals,
known tech stack, industry breach history). Not a scare tactic — a credibility builder.
Note: "This is what your environment looks like from the outside.
Happy to show you what it looks like from the inside. — [Your name, phone number]"
```

### Timing and Follow-Up

Send on a Tuesday or Wednesday. Expect delivery Thursday or Monday.

The day after estimated delivery: send a LinkedIn message.
```
"[Name] — sent you something in the mail this week. Wanted to make sure it landed.
Happy to connect on what I sent."
```

Two days after LinkedIn message: make the call.
```
"Hey [Name] — I sent you [describe the artifact] last week.
The reason I sent it specifically to you was [the personal connection].
Did you get a chance to look at it?"
```
